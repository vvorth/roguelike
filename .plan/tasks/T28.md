# T28 — Bump-to-attack, NPC turns, targeting, levelling and death
Status: todo
Depends on: T22, T23, T24, T25, T26, T27 (all of version 5's other modules)

## Goal
Wire the whole of version 5 into the turn loop: walking into a monster attacks it, monsters take
their turns, the player can fire a bow, kills award experience, levelling raises stats, and
running out of hit points ends the run.

## Context
This is an interactive terminal roguelike engine with ASCII graphics, similar in feel to ADOM.
Versions 1–4 are complete and passing **1982 tests**: a procedurally generated multi-level
dungeon, field of view, fog of war, doors, colour, diagonal movement, pathfinding and automatic
navigation.

You are the last worker in version 5. Every other module is finished: statistics and derived
formulas, weapons, status effects, point-to-point line of sight, key bindings, event wording,
combat arithmetic, monsters with AI and spawning, and the renderer. **None of it is connected to
anything.** You connect it.

The engine is built by independent workers who never communicate; a frozen contract defines
every seam. You are the only worker running now, so nothing is changing underneath you.

**`game.py` is the only module allowed to import this widely, and it is the only module that
holds game rules.** `step` and `advance` are **pure** — same state in, same state out, no
terminal, no clock, no I/O, no randomness that is not derived from the state itself. `run` owns
the keyboard and the clock and holds no rules. That separation is what makes the entire game
testable headless, and version 5 must not weaken it.

### The rule that is easiest to break

**A rejected move consumes no turn.** This has been version 1's headline rule through four
increments. Version 5 adds a consequence: if no turn is consumed, **the world does not tick** —
monsters do not act, poison does not burn, nothing regenerates. Walking into a wall must leave
the world exactly as it was.

### The randomness rule

**No `random.Random` may be stored on `GameState`, `Player` or `NPC`.** A stored generator is
mutable, and two states built from one parent by `replace()` would share and corrupt a single
stream — precisely what the frozen-dataclass discipline exists to prevent. Every roll derives a
fresh generator from `roll_seed(master_seed, turns, actor_id, salt)` (§0.12). The player is
permanently `actor_id 0`. Salts are fixed: `1` to-hit, `2` damage, `3` status, `4` AI wander.

### The v4 seam that becomes live

Version 4 shipped `interruption(before, after)` returning `None` in every case, because the
conditions the user named — *"seeing a hostile, receiving damage, character state change"* —
needed monsters and hit points. **Both now exist, so you implement it** (§7.14). Without it,
auto-explore walks into a jackal pack and keeps walking; since **two jackals beat a baseline
player 100% of the time** (600 simulated fights, zero wins), that is a guaranteed death with no
chance to react.

Note the amendment: the interruption event is **appended** to the turn's events, not substituted
for them. Version 4 replaced them, which was harmless while the function always returned `None`;
substituting now would throw away `The jackal hits you.` in favour of a bare `You stop.`

Out of scope, explicitly — do not build, do not stub: item pickup or drop, ammo counts, armour,
critical hits, monster infighting, fleeing, save/load, a message history pane, auto-fight, mouse
input, or depth-scaled spawning. Extra surface is a defect, not initiative.

Coordinates are `(x, y)`, origin top-left, `y` grows **down**.

### `advance_npcs` — one world-tick, in this order
Called by `step` and by `advance`, **only after an action that consumed a turn**.

1. **Status effects and regeneration first**, for the player and every NPC. Poison damage can
   kill. If the player dies here, stop — remaining NPCs do not act.
2. **Then every NPC acts**, in `actor_id` order, by the energy rule: `energy += speed`, then
   `while energy >= ENERGY_THRESHOLD: act; energy -= ENERGY_THRESHOLD`.
3. Dead NPCs are removed from `state.npcs` and their `xp_value` credited.
4. Events are filtered and capped (§16.1).

The player's field of view is recomputed by `_take_turn` as it always has been. **NPC movement
does not recompute it** — moving monsters do not change what terrain is visible.

The `WANDERING` → `HUNTING` transition and the `FORGET_TICKS` revert happen **here**, not in
`npc.plan_action`, which is pure and returns only an action. T26's report states precisely what
it expects; follow it.

### `interruption` — all three conditions, in priority order
1. **A hostile comes into view** — an NPC position in `after.visible` not in `before.visible`.
   Return `Event(SPOTTED_HOSTILE, name=<species name>)`, choosing the nearest by Chebyshev
   distance, ties broken by coordinate.
2. **The player took damage** — `after` hp < `before` hp. Return `Event(INTERRUPTED)`.
3. **Character state changed** — the player's `status_effects` gained a kind not present before.
   Return `Event(INTERRUPTED)`.

Otherwise `None`. Opening a door still does **not** interrupt, and neither does a
already-visible NPC merely moving.

### Levelling — the exact loop
```
xp_to_next(L) = 25 * L * L
while xp >= xp_to_next(level):
    xp -= xp_to_next(level)          # note the SUBTRACTION
    level += 1
    vit += 1
    if level is odd:  str_ += 1
    else:             agi += 1
    hp += (new max_hp - old max_hp)  # NOT a full heal
```
Derived stats are recomputed through `stats.derive` — there is no second HP formula.

### The message cap (§16.1)
Cap `events` at `MAX_EVENTS` (3), keeping the highest priority. Priority, highest first:
`PLAYER_DIED` › `LEVELLED_UP`/`NPC_KILLED` › `NPC_HIT_PLAYER`/`POISONED`/`POISON_DAMAGE` ›
`PLAYER_HIT_NPC`/`PLAYER_MISSED_NPC` › everything else. Within a band, emission order is kept.
Events for NPCs **outside `visible`** are dropped **before** the cap — except `NPC_HIT_PLAYER`
and `POISONED`, which are always kept because the player always perceives themselves.

## Acceptance criteria

**Movement and bump-to-attack**
- Every existing `try_move` behaviour is unchanged with a default `occupied` — run the existing
  movement suite's cases again.
- A move into a cell in `occupied` returns `moved=False`, `blocked_by_npc=<that cell>`, and the
  position unchanged.
- Destination with both a closed door and an NPC → `blocked_by_npc` wins.
- `step` on a `MOVE` into an NPC resolves a melee attack: a turn is consumed, the player does
  **not** move, and `PLAYER_HIT_NPC` or `PLAYER_MISSED_NPC` is emitted.
- **Walking into a wall still consumes no turn AND does not tick the world** — assert `turns`,
  every NPC's position and `energy`, and the player's hp are all identical afterwards. This is
  the headline regression test.

**NPC turns and energy**
- A baseline NPC (`speed 100`) acts exactly once per world-tick.
- A `speed 180` NPC acts **twice** in some ticks and once in others; over 10 ticks it acts 18
  times. A `speed 80` NPC acts 8 times over 10 ticks.
- NPCs act in `actor_id` order, deterministically.
- An NPC adjacent to the player attacks it; the player's hp drops and `NPC_HIT_PLAYER` (or
  `NPC_MISSED_PLAYER`) is emitted.
- **NPCs on other levels are frozen**: descend, take several turns, ascend — the NPCs on the
  first level are exactly where and how they were, with the same energy.
- NPCs never occupy the same cell as each other or the player.
- `advance_npcs` on a state with no NPCs returns it unchanged.

**Status, regeneration, death**
- Poison ticks once per world-tick, damages by `magnitude`, expires after `remaining_turns`, and
  ticks even for an actor whose energy did not cross the threshold.
- The player regains 1 hp every `REGEN_TURNS` ticks, capped at `max_hp`. **NPCs do not
  regenerate.**
- **Death by poison ends the run identically to death by a blow**: `running` False, `outcome`
  set from the message table, and no separate code path — assert both causes reach the same end
  state shape.
- When the player dies during the status phase, remaining NPCs do **not** act that tick.

**Targeting**
- `FIRE` with no valid target emits `NO_TARGET` and consumes **no** turn.
- `FIRE` with targets sets `Targeting`, emits `TARGETING`, consumes no turn.
- `TARGET_NEXT` cycles and wraps; consumes no turn.
- `FIRE` while targeting resolves a ranged attack with `strength_applies=False`, consumes a
  turn, and clears `targeting`.
- **Any other key while targeting** clears it, consumes the command entirely: no turn, no action,
  no event change.
- The target list is **exactly** `visible ∩ npc positions` within `ranged.range`, sorted by
  `(distance, coord)`. Assert an NPC that is in range but **not visible** is excluded.
- Targeting does not survive a level change; a target that dies before `FIRE` resolves cancels
  the shot with no turn.

**Levelling**
- `xp_to_next(1) == 25`, `(2) == 100`, `(3) == 225`.
- Killing an NPC credits exactly its `xp_value` and emits `NPC_KILLED`.
- Reaching 25 xp at level 1 gives level 2, `vit +1`, `str_ +1` (odd), and hp raised by exactly
  the max-hp delta — **not** a full heal. Assert current hp explicitly.
- Level 3 raises `agi`, not `str_`.
- **A single kill crossing two thresholds levels twice**, with xp correctly carried over — this
  is the off-by-one the research draft got wrong.
- `LEVELLED_UP` carries the new level.

**Interruption**
- An activity is cancelled when a hostile newly enters view, emitting `SPOTTED_HOSTILE` with the
  species name.
- An activity is cancelled when the player takes damage, and when the player is newly poisoned.
- **The interruption event is appended, not substituted** — assert that both
  `The jackal hits you.` and the stop message survive into the rendered line.
- An already-visible NPC merely moving does **not** interrupt. Opening a door does **not**
  interrupt.

**Messages**
- The cap holds at `MAX_EVENTS` with six NPCs acting; priority order is respected;
  `NPC_HIT_PLAYER` from an unseen NPC is **kept**; `PLAYER_HIT_NPC` for an unseen NPC is dropped.

**Purity and determinism**
- `step` and `advance` never mutate the input state; `state.saved` is rebuilt.
- **The same seed and the same key sequence produce an identical final state** — assert over a
  scripted run of 50+ commands including fights, and across two `PYTHONHASHSEED` values in a
  `subprocess`.
- **No `Random` is stored on any state** — assert by inspecting the dataclass fields.
- `game.py` contains no `time.sleep` and no clock read — assert with `ast`.

**Chrome**
- `format_stats` produces the §7.13 layout with two-space separators and real values.
