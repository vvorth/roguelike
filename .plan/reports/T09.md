## How corridor routing changed (for the integrator's end-to-end test)

Corridors are no longer centre-to-centre L-doglegs. A room's **ring box** is its floor rect
grown by one cell (floor + wall ring); because G5's `intersects(margin=1)` rejection is
*exactly* a ring-box overlap test, ring boxes are pairwise disjoint. Corridors are now routed
by breadth-first search over a graph of **free cells** (interior cells outside every ring box)
plus **whole-room nodes**, where the only edges touching a room node are its doors — so a
corridor can only reach a wall perpendicularly, through a non-corner perimeter cell whose
outward neighbour is free space. G8 still holds structurally: every route starts from a cell
or room already connected to `rooms[0]` and ends at the room being joined, so the carved set
is one connected region by induction; a room the router cannot reach is **dropped from the
level** rather than carved and left stranded, and `_assert_guarantees` re-verifies G8 with a
flood fill before any `Level` is returned.

Assumptions made:
- **Why G9a-d and G4a are structural, not checked-after-the-fact.** Corridors are carved only
  in free space, doors only at non-corner ring cells whose mouth is free. So a door's inward
  neighbour is room floor and its outward neighbour is corridor (G9c); the two cells flanking
  it along the wall lie in the same room's ring box, which no corridor may carve and no other
  room can own, and the router refuses two orthogonally adjacent doors on one room, so they
  stay `WALL` (G9b). Doors of *different* rooms can never be adjacent either — a door's mouth
  must be free space, and another room's doors are inside that room's disjoint ring box (G9d).
  Since free space never touches a room floor, **every** way into a room is a door (G4a).
- **A room the router cannot reach is dropped.** Two rooms whose wall rings *touch* (floor
  rects exactly 2 apart, which G5 permits) can never be joined under the tightened G9: the one
  cell between their floors belongs to both rings, so making it a door of one leaves an
  unmarked gap in the other, and making it a door of both breaks G9d. Dropping is legal —
  `max_rooms` is a ceiling and G4 needs only one room — and is the only alternative to an
  unreachable pocket (G8).
- **`_lay_out` redraws the whole layout up to `_LAYOUT_ATTEMPTS` (8) times**, keeping the best,
  and stops as soon as a draw strands nothing. This is why room density did not regress:
  measured over 200 seeds at 80x22 the level now averages **10.92 rooms with zero stranded**,
  against the v1 build's 10.70. It also repairs the degenerate thin maps, where a single draw
  strands a lot: at 80x6, 3.50 -> 5.99 rooms per level. Residual stranding is 0 per level at
  (80,22), (60,40), (100,40), (30,30) and (25,25); it is 0.02 at (40,15) and only the
  pathological (6,22) / (80,6) shapes still lose a fraction of a room per level.
- **`_place_rooms` is byte-for-byte unchanged.** I deliberately did not widen G5's rejection
  margin to guarantee a routing lane: measured, `margin=2` costs ~29% of the rooms at the
  default size (10.9 -> 7.7) to fix map shapes nobody plays. Room placement was not the defect.
- **RNG draws changed, as the brief allows.** One `rng.shuffle` per room over its door
  candidates (varying which door and which of several equally short routes wins), replacing v1's
  one `getrandbits(1)` per corridor. `_place_rooms` still draws first and identically, so its
  stream is untouched; corridors differ, so levels differ from v1. No test is pinned to a v1
  grid snapshot. Still one local `random.Random(seed)`, no module-level `random.*`, no
  `time`/`os.urandom`/`id()`/`hash()`, and no set or dict iteration order reaching the output —
  the BFS queue is a list, `visited` is a dict used only for membership, and the only `set`s
  are membership-only.
- **A corridor may still pass through a room** (in one door, out another) and corridors may
  merge; both were true in v1 and remain legal.
- **`_assert_guarantees` raises `RuntimeError`**, unchanged from T02, and now also covers
  G9a-G9d and G4a via the new `_assert_doors`. I verified it is not vacuous: hand-mutated
  levels reproducing each violation class (flank carved open, door in open space, three
  adjacent doors, a room stripped of doors, a door walled off) are each rejected, while the
  legal case of two doors 2 apart with a corridor cell between them is accepted.
- **A single-room level is asserted to have zero doors**, matching G4a's exemption; that also
  covers a level that lost every other room to dropping.
