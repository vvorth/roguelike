## The five traps, each one handled

1. **A rejected move consumes no turn and the world does not tick.** Untouched: every new
   command goes through `_tick_world(state, ...)`, which compares turn counters, so `PICK_UP`
   on bare floor, on an emptied chest, or with a full pack ticks nothing. Pinned by
   `test_a_refused_pick_up_does_not_tick_the_world` and
   `test_opening_the_inventory_never_ticks_the_world`, alongside every surviving v1–v5 test.
2. **`tick_effects` returns three values.** `_tick_status` now unpacks
   `(effects, damage, healing)`, applies both, caps healing at `derive(stats).max_hp`, and
   still returns only the damage — damage is the half that can kill and the half that has an
   event. `test_poison_and_regeneration_are_told_apart_rather_than_netted` asserts 2 burned
   and 2 mended reads as `POISON_DAMAGE` at unchanged HP, not as silence.
3. **`equip` raises on a `Consumable`.** `_use_or_equip` dispatches on `isinstance` *before*
   calling `equip`; nothing catches a `ValueError` anywhere.
   `test_e_on_a_consumable_drinks_it_rather_than_raising`.
4. **Monsters are placed after chests** (§27.4). `_populate(level, depth)` places the chest
   first and then spawns, and since `spawn_npcs` takes no forbidden-cell set, a monster that
   lands on the chest is **dropped** — the same answer `spawn_npcs` itself gives when it has
   no legal cell (CONTRACT-v5 §11 v5), rather than relaxing anything.
   `test_no_monster_ever_starts_on_a_chest` sweeps 120 seeds and asserts the sweep actually
   produced chests to check.
5. **`Player.melee`/`ranged` moved into `Player.inventory`.** Four call sites in `game.py`,
   two assertions in `test_game.py`; the full suite is the check and it is green.

## Decisions I had to make, and why

### The one genuine contradiction in the contract — resolved, not worked around

**§5 v6 says the letters `a`–`t` select a carried item; §7.17 says `e` equips or uses the
selection and `d` drops it.** Both cannot be true of the same keystroke: if `d` selected the
fourth item there would be no key left to drop anything with. §7.17 describes the screen the
player actually operates, so the two action keys win and the item labels skip them:

```python
ITEM_LETTERS = "abcfghijklmnopqrstuv"     # a-v minus d and e; 20 labels for CARRY_LIMIT 20
```

What is printed beside an item is exactly the key that selects it, so nothing is hidden and no
item is unreachable; eighteen of the twenty are still inside §5 v6's `a`–`t`. **This is the one
place I read the contract against its own letter, and it is reported rather than assumed** — if
the orchestrator prefers strict `a`–`t` selection, the cost is that items 4 and 5 become
unselectable, and the change is one constant.

### The rest, none of them contradictions — just silences

- **The block chance passed to `resolve_attack` is decided once**, in `_shield_block(shield,
  missile, defender_agi, attacker_agi)`: `0` with no shield, the plain `block_chance` for a
  blow, `combat.ranged_block_chance(...)` for a missile. **No shield is 0, never the floor of
  5** — passing `ranged_block_chance(0, ...)` would invent a 5% block for every monster, none
  of which carries a shield. The missile case has no live caller in v6 (§23.6 records that);
  it is written and tested rather than guessed at two call sites.
- **`CHEST_OPENED` is the first item out of a closed chest; `PICKED_UP` is every item after.**
  §16 v6 provides both sentences and each says one thing; emitting both at once reads *"The
  chest holds: dagger You pick up the dagger."*
- **`CHEST_EMPTY` costs no turn.** §7.16 charges a turn for *taking one item*; there is none to
  take, so it is the same rule as the empty cell beside it.
- **Dropping costs a turn.** §7.17 names equipping and drinking as costing one and opening,
  browsing and closing as costing none; dropping is plainly an action rather than a look.
- **`e` and `d` close the screen** as well as acting. A turn has passed and the world has moved;
  what the player needs to see next is the map. Browsing and closing stay free.
- **A letter with no item behind it closes**, like any other key — an empty pack answering to
  almost nothing (most of the alphabet is an item letter) reads as a stuck screen.
- **The chest is drawn from its own stream** off the level's seed, via
  `roll_seed(level.seed, 0, 0, _SALT_CHEST)`, not from the generator `spawn_npcs` draws from.
  Both are equally reproducible; sharing one would have made the chest's very first "is there a
  chest?" draw shift **every monster on every level**, silently re-rolling the whole v5
  bestiary layout for no gain. `test_placing_a_chest_does_not_disturb_where_the_monsters_go`
  pins it.
- **Bare hands are a `Weapon`**, `BARE_HANDS` (1–2 BLUNT, `strength_applies=True`), public in
  `__all__`. It is in `game.py` and not `items.py` because §7.15 makes "what bare-handed means"
  this module's rule — and expressing it as a weapon means punching goes down the identical
  path, resistance included: a giant bat is VULNERABLE to BLUNT, so fists beat the dagger on it.
- **`f` with no bow reports `NO_TARGET`**, per §7.15's "`NO_TARGET`-style refusal". There is no
  separate "you have no bow" wording and no turn is spent finding out.
- **`run` hands the raw key to `inventory_key(state, key)`** while the screen is open, instead of
  translating it. It is forced: §5 v6 requires `e`, `d` and the item letters to have no
  `CommandKind`, so `translate_key` flattens all of them to one `UNKNOWN`. `inventory_key` is
  pure and holds every rule; `run` gained a routing decision and no rule. `step` still answers a
  *command* arriving while the screen is open by closing it, no turn — defensive, and tested.
- **`CHEST_HERE` fires on a within-level step onto the cell**, not on arriving by staircase,
  where `ASCENDED`/`DESCENDED` is the news. A chest *can* share a cell with a down-staircase
  (`place_chest` keeps its distance from `player_start` and from doors, not from stairs), and
  both are then reported.
- **The look cursor says nothing about a chest.** `describe_cell` reports the terrain under it.
  §7.18 asks only for the step-on message and `events.py` has no chest terrain word; inventing
  one here would be composing wording in the wrong module.

## What I did *not* change

`movement.py` has **no code change** — only a docstring paragraph recording why. Items, chests,
damage types, resistances and shields are all rules about what a bump *does*, never about
whether a step is allowed; a chest is not an obstacle (you stand on it to open it) so it never
joins `occupied`, and there is no `Tile.CHEST`. `MoveResult` keeps four fields, `try_move` keeps
six parameters, and the module keeps its two imports — now asserted by three new tests so a
later increment that grows a fifth collision case has to say so out loud.

## Things in other modules that surprised me — reported, not fixed

1. **`spawn_npcs` has no forbidden-cell parameter**, so §27.4's "treat the chest cell the way it
   treats any other occupied cell" can only be honoured by dropping a monster that lands there.
   Nothing is wrong — the amendment is satisfiable — but the mechanism is subtraction after the
   fact rather than exclusion during placement, and if a later increment wants several chests or
   items on the floor, an `occupied` parameter on `spawn_npcs` is the clean fix.
2. **`place_chest` will happily put a chest on a down-staircase.** `_is_legal_cell` excludes
   doors and cells within 8 of `player_start`; stairs are ordinary walkable floor to it. Harmless
   — both are reported and both work — but it is not obviously intended by §27.2.
3. **T30's noted `SPOTTED_HOSTILE` wording mismatch is still live** (implemented `"There is a
   {name} in view."` vs CONTRACT-v5 §16's `"A {name} comes into view!"`). Confirmed still
   present; out of scope for me as it was for T30.
4. **`ranged_block_chance` still has no live caller** after wiring, exactly as §23.6 predicted:
   nothing shoots at the player and no monster owns a shield. `_shield_block` is where it would
   be called from, and its missile case is tested directly.
5. **`NPC_SHIELD_BLOCKED` is likewise unreachable in play** for the same reason. It is emitted at
   the one site that could ever say it (`_player_attack`, on `result.blocked`), so the wiring
   exists the day a shielded monster does.
