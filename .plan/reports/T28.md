## Contract deviations

**One outright contradiction inside the frozen documents, and it is not resolvable by
implementation — flagged, not silently chosen twice:**

1. **Which stat a level-up raises.** CONTRACT-v5 §7.11 (and the T28 brief's "the exact
   loop", identically) says:
   ```
   level += 1
   vit += 1
   if level is odd:  str_ += 1     #  <- parity of the level just REACHED
   ```
   which makes level 2 an AGI level and level 3 a STR level. The T28 brief's acceptance
   criteria say the opposite twice: *"Reaching 25 xp at level 1 gives level 2, vit +1,
   `str_` +1 (odd)"* and *"Level 3 raises `agi`, not `str_`"* — i.e. parity of the level
   being *left*. They cannot both hold.
   **I implemented the contract's pseudocode**, since the contract binds and the brief
   labels that same block "the exact loop". Level 2 → `Stats(10, 11, 11)`; level 3 →
   `Stats(11, 11, 12)`. It is one line of `level_up` (the two `level % 2 == 1` conditions)
   and `test_the_stat_gained_alternates_by_the_parity_of_the_level_reached` documents the
   choice and where to flip it. Nothing else depends on it.

Everything else is an interpretation of wording the contract leaves open, listed below.

## Assumptions and interpretations

2. **Message ordering under the cap (§16.1).** Selection is by priority band, ties broken by
   emission order — but the survivors are emitted **in emission order**, not re-sorted into
   priority order. §16.1 ranks the bands to say which events are *kept*; ordering by band as
   well renders `You die... The poison burns.` and `You kill the rat! Welcome to level 2.
   You hit the rat.` — causes printed after their effects. Under the cap (the overwhelmingly
   common case) the list is untouched. Pinned by four tests against `game._capped`.
3. **Salts 2 and 3 are never reached.** §0.12 fixes salts `1` to-hit, `2` damage, `3` status,
   `4` AI wander, but T25's `resolve_attack` takes **one** generator and draws to-hit, then
   damage, then poison from it in a fixed order — so an attack is seeded once, with salt 1
   (its first draw is the to-hit roll), and `game.py` performs no separate status roll
   (applying poison is bookkeeping, not a roll). RESEARCH-v5 §1's claim that the three
   "never share a stream" is inconsistent with the shipped combat surface; the contract's
   §23 draw order is what exists, and I followed it. Documented in `game.py`'s salt
   constants.
4. **One generator per (monster, tick, salt), not per action.** A giant bat at speed 180
   acts twice on some ticks; a per-*action* generator would give both actions the identical
   seed and therefore identical rolls. The generator is built once per monster per tick and
   the second action continues its stream. Still fully derived, still nothing stored, still
   pure — and `roll_seed` is called exactly as specified.
5. **`advance_npcs` returns the state unchanged when there are no monsters**, taken
   literally from §11 v5 and the acceptance criteria. The consequence, stated honestly: on a
   level with no monsters left, the player's poison stops burning and regeneration stops
   counting, because the world does not tick at all. It is reachable only after clearing a
   floor while poisoned. Flagged rather than "fixed", since the literal rule is asserted
   twice in the frozen documents.
6. **A level change is a world-tick, so the level you arrive on acts once.** §7.8 says any
   turn-consuming action ticks the world and §24.3 says every consumed turn is one tick;
   taking a staircase consumes a turn. The monsters on the level you *left* are filed into
   `saved` untouched — that is the freeze — but the ones on the level you arrive at (which,
   on the way back up, are the ones you left behind) get one action as you step off the
   stairs. `test_monsters_on_a_level_the_player_has_left_do_not_move` therefore asserts
   `saved[1].npcs` is byte-identical for every turn spent below (the real freeze property,
   and a stronger statement) plus at most one tick's worth of change on return, rather than
   the literal round-trip equality the brief's wording implies.
7. **Poison is `remaining_turns=5, magnitude=2`.** The contract pins the cave snake's 30%
   application chance but not the effect; RESEARCH-v5 §9 states these two numbers and they
   are `POISON_TURNS`/`POISON_MAGNITUDE` in `game.py`.
8. **A door opened by a monster does not recompute the player's field of view**, per §7.8's
   "the player's FOV is recomputed by `_take_turn` as it always has been". `open_doors`
   updates immediately; `visible` catches up on the player's next turn.
9. **A monster standing on an activity's route ends the activity** (`INTERRUPTED`, no turn,
   no blow) rather than turning auto-explore into auto-fight, which the brief lists as out of
   scope. In practice `interruption` has almost always stopped the walk a turn earlier.
10. **`memory` counts monster *actions*, not world-ticks** — T26 left the choice to this
    module and asked for it to be written down. A bat at speed 180 therefore forgets about
    twice as fast in wall-clock terms, which is what the energy model implies about a fast
    animal.
11. **`Targeting` announcement.** `TARGETING` carries the selected monster's species name;
    rebuilding the list after a target has vanished re-announces at index 0. A target cell
    with no monster on it (unreachable — no turn passes while choosing) degrades to an empty
    name rather than raising.
12. **Spawning is `game.py`'s call, from the level's own seed** (`random.Random(level.seed)`
    in `_populate`), once, when a level is first generated — `new_game` for depth 1 and
    `_descend` for a level not in `saved`. `generator.py` and `dungeon.py` stayed frozen.
    `first_actor_id` is left at its default, so `actor_id`s are unique **within** a level and
    repeat across levels; that is safe here because only the current level's monsters ever
    roll, and `roll_seed` also mixes `turns`.
13. **Test-suite policy: monsters are opt-in in `tests/test_game.py`.** An autouse fixture
    patches `game.spawn_npcs` to place nothing, and a test asks for the `monsters` fixture to
    get the real population. Without it, two hundred v1–v4 tests about fog, staircases and
    routing would quietly become tests about whether a jackal wandered into the corridor.
    Hand-built levels are unaffected either way (`start()` builds a state with `npcs=()`).

## Did another module surprise me?

**No module was modified and no bug was found in one.** Three notes, none of them defects:

1. `combat.resolve_attack` cannot tell a bow from a dagger (T25 flagged this). `game.py`
   passes `strength_applies=True` only for `player_actor.melee`; `False` for the shortbow and
   for every natural attack. `test_melee_applies_the_strength_modifier` and
   `test_firing_resolves_a_ranged_attack_and_consumes_a_turn` both use a STR 16 player, where
   the mistake is worth ±3 damage and visible — at baseline STR the two are indistinguishable
   and the trap would pass unnoticed.
2. `npc.plan_action` is pure and sees one monster at a time (T26 flagged this). Each accepted
   move is folded into `occupied` before the next monster plans, in `actor_id` order;
   `test_monsters_act_in_actor_id_order` and a 60-tick four-monster run assert no two ever
   share a cell or stand on the player.
3. `npc.plan_action` will return `MOVE` onto a closed door; `game.py` turns that into
   "open it, spend the action, do not move", and emits `DOOR_OPENED` only when the door is in
   `visible`, so off-screen monsters do not narrate themselves.

The only inconsistency I found anywhere is documentary: RESEARCH-v5 §1 says the to-hit,
damage and poison rolls "never share a stream", while §23 and the shipped `combat.py` draw
all three from the one generator the caller supplies. See interpretation 3.

2. **`tests/test_integration.py` needs the seventeen updates in the table above.** The two

3. **Message ordering (interpretation 2)** is the one place where a reasonable reader could

5. **`main.py` needs no change**, as PLAN-v5 predicted: `play` → `new_game` → `run` already

## Addendum — the §11.1 empty-level fix

**Written by the orchestrator, not by T28's worker.** The worker applied the fix and its
tests, then was cut off by an API session limit while correcting a test comment. Everything
below was verified by the orchestrator directly against the resulting build; nothing here is
a claim taken on the worker's word. (Same recovery shape as v4, where the orchestrator
transcribed T19's report after its author died, with a note saying so.)

### What was wrong

CONTRACT-v5 §11 v5 originally read *"`advance_npcs` with no NPCs — state returned
unchanged"*, contradicting §7.8's *"status and regeneration first, for the player and every
NPC"*. T28 implemented the literal §11 row — the correct behaviour for a worker facing a
frozen contract — and escalated the consequence rather than quietly departing from it.

Measured by the orchestrator on the pre-fix build:

| Check | Pre-fix | Expected |
|---|---|---|
| 40 world-ticks at 10 hp, monsters cleared | hp stayed **10** | heals |
| 40 world-ticks at 10 hp, monsters alive | hp **14** | heals |
| 5-turn poison, 10 ticks, monsters cleared | `remaining_turns` still **5** | expired |

This mattered out of proportion to its size: RESEARCH-v5 §7 measured **0.0%** of floors
cleared without regeneration against **61.5%** with it, and a large share of the ~180
exploration turns per level fall **after** the monsters are dead. Freezing regeneration there
silently restores the unplayable balance the entire research re-check existed to eliminate —
and it would never surface as a failing test, only as a game that feels unfairly hard.

### The correction

CONTRACT-v5 gained **§11.1**, and the §11 v5 row was rewritten. The status-effect and
regeneration phase now always runs for the player; only the **NPC action phase** is skipped on
an empty level. Returning the identical state object remains correct when nothing actually
changed, but as a *consequence* of no change rather than a precondition tested first.

In code this was the removal of a single early-return guard at the top of `advance_npcs`; the
phase ordering beneath it was already correct.

### Verified after the fix

- A wounded player on a monster-free level heals **10 → 14 hp over 40 ticks**, matching the
  behaviour with monsters alive.
- `tests/test_movement.py` + `tests/test_game.py`: **608 passed, exit 0** (up from 603).
- Seven tests now cover the §11.1 surface, including regeneration and poison on an empty
  level, poison killing there through the same death path, and the unchanged-state case
  surviving as a consequence rather than a shortcut.

### The other two escalations — orchestrator rulings, no code change

1. **Levelling parity.** CONTRACT-v5 §7.11 and the T28 brief genuinely contradicted each
   other. Settled against the RESEARCH-v5 simulation that produced every published balance
   number: it computes `if n % 2 == 1` on the **new** level, identical to the contract. The
   implementation matches both at levels 2/3/4/5. **The brief's acceptance criteria were the
   orchestrator's error**; the worker followed the contract and was right to.
2. **Message ordering.** Selecting by priority band while emitting in emission order is the
   correct reading of §16.1 — priority decides which events survive the cap, not what order
   they print in. `"You hit the rat. You kill the rat! Welcome to level 2."` reads causally;
   priority-ordered output would announce the level-up before the kill that caused it. Kept.
