## Contract deviations

**None.** CONTRACT-v5 §14 v5 is implemented exactly: observer's cell centre as the eye
(doubled `(2*ox+1, 2*oy+1)`), the existing `_SAMPLE_OFFSETS` on the target cell, the
existing `_segment_is_clear` for every segment, opacity snapshotted over the bounding box
of the two cells grown by one cell and clipped to the level, `(a, a) -> True`,
out-of-bounds `-> False`, argument order `(observer, target)`.

**One factual note on the brief, not on the contract.** The brief's acceptance criterion
says of the one-cell margin: *"assert a case that fails without it."* No such case exists,
and I could not manufacture one — this is a measurement, not an opinion:

- I instrumented the opacity dict `_segment_is_clear` is handed and recorded every key it
  looks up, over ~2.6 M segment traversals (all cell pairs of 200 random 16×16 levels at
  35% wall density, all eight samples each). **Zero lookups fell outside the two cells'
  *tight* bounding rectangle.**
- I then ran a tight-box (margin 0) variant of `has_line_of_sight` against the shipped
  margin-1 version, exhaustively over all cell pairs of 400 random levels up to 9×9, and
  against `compute_visible` on five generated 80×22 levels. **Zero disagreements.**
- The reason is structural: a segment to a sample point stays inside the two cells'
  bounding rectangle, and at a lattice crossing the diagonal rule's flanking cells
  `(cx+step_x, cy)` and `(cx, cy+step_y)` mix an x and a y that both belong to cells the
  segment genuinely enters — so, the box being a rectangle, they are inside it too.

So the margin the contract mandates is a **guarantee with a cell to spare**, not a knife
edge. I implemented it as specified (erring outward is the safe direction, and it is what
`compute_visible` does), and pinned it with a test that asserts the exact set of cells
`world.is_transparent` is consulted about — that test *does* fail if the box is shrunk to
the tight rectangle or grown to the radius disc. A second test,
`test_the_margin_is_a_guarantee_with_slack_not_a_knife_edge`, records the measurement
above so the next reader does not have to rediscover it. The criterion's other half —
*"construct a case where the clear line bulges outside their tight bounding rectangle"* —
is satisfied literally and is asserted (see below).

## Assumptions made

1. **Out of bounds beats reflexivity.** `has_line_of_sight(level, doors, a, a)` with `a`
   off-map returns `False`, not `True`: §11 v5 says out-of-bounds never raises and is
   `False`, and a cell that does not exist cannot see itself. Asserted in
   `test_out_of_bounds_beats_the_reflexive_rule`.
2. **"Bounding box grown by one" means grown in cell space, then clipped to the level** —
   `[min(ox,tx)-1 .. max(ox,tx)+1] × [min(oy,ty)-1 .. max(oy,ty)+1] ∩ level`, matching how
   `compute_visible` clips its own snapshot. Pinned by the `consulted_cells` spy tests.
3. **The regression guard on `compute_visible` is structural, not a captured blob.** Exact
   visible sets rendered as pictures (three hand levels, including a cluttered room with
   nine pillars, cell for cell), exact counts at radii 3/5/20, the mirror/transpose
   symmetry of a square room, the full wall ring from every cell of that room, exact
   visible-cell counts for 44 origins across two *generated* levels, and door-open versus
   door-closed pictures with identical coordinates. Every number was captured from the
   pre-v5 implementation; any behavioural drift fails here before it reaches
   `tests/test_activity.py`.
4. The generator is deterministic and frozen, so `generate_level(1)` / `generate_level(2)`
   are stable fixtures; the pinned counts and the pinned asymmetric pair depend on that.
5. `tests/test_fov.py` may import `roguelike.generator` (it did not before). The
   acceptance criteria require generated levels; `fov.py` itself still imports only
   `roguelike.level` and `roguelike.world`, asserted with `ast` as before.

### The asymmetric pair

**On a generated level** — `generate_level(1)`, `open_doors = frozenset()`:

```
has_line_of_sight(level, frozenset(), (54, 16), (57, 9))  ->  True
has_line_of_sight(level, frozenset(), (57, 9), (54, 16))  ->  False
```

`(54, 16)` is `FLOOR`, `(57, 9)` is `WALL`. `compute_visible` agrees in both directions:
`(57, 9) in compute_visible(..., (54, 16), 20)` is `True`, and
`(54, 16) in compute_visible(..., (57, 9), 20)` is `False` — so the asymmetry is
`compute_visible`'s own, faithfully reproduced, not something the new function invented.
Two further pairs on the same level, if the integrator wants more:
`(36, 13) / (42, 10)` and `(12, 5) / (22, 4)` (the latter reversed: the wall cell sees the
floor cell, not the other way round).

**On a hand-built level** — a 7×7 open level with a single wall at `(4, 1)`:

```
.......
....#..
.......      observer (0, 0) sees (6, 2);  (6, 2) does NOT see (0, 0)
.......
```

This same pair is also the required **bulge case**: the only one of the eight sample
points of `(6, 2)` with a clear line is its **bottom-left corner `(6, 3)`**, which lies
strictly below the rectangle spanned by the two cell centres `(0.5, 0.5)`–`(6.5, 2.5)`.
The winning line therefore leaves that rectangle, an implementation that boxed the two
*points* instead of the two *cells* would clip the sample point away, and the answer still
matches `compute_visible` (asserted, plus agreement over every cell pair of that level).

Rate check: sampling 2000 random cell pairs on `generate_level(1)` finds a handful of
disagreements — consistent with RESEARCH-v5 §8's 0.28%. The test asserts
`0 < disagreements < 5%` so it pins the phenomenon without pinning a fragile exact count.

   box down to the tight rectangle, every behavioural test will still pass and only
   `test_opacity_is_snapshotted_over_the_bounding_box_grown_by_one` will fail. That test

   swap is otherwise wrong just 0.28% of the time. Worth a second pair of eyes at review.

   `PERCEPTION_RADIUS` (§24.2) is entirely T26's to apply, before or after the call.

   temptation exists.
5. `tests/test_fov.py` now imports `roguelike.generator`. If the integrator wants test
